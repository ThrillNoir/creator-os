# Caption principles

## The test

A caption is ready only when the creator or their manager can read it and say, “Yes, that sounds like me.”

## Rules

- Write from known context; never invent a feeling, event, relationship, location, or personal detail.
- Prefer one natural observation over a stack of hooks, adjectives, and calls to action.
- Use the creator’s vocabulary and rhythm, including restraint.
- Do not turn embedded SEO descriptions into social captions.
- Use research to choose relevant angles—not to force every trend into the post.
- Tailor the draft to the actual destination account and audience.
- Avoid generic AI phrases, motivational filler, rhetorical-question templates, and unnecessary em dashes.
- Avoid repeating recent openings, calls to action, emojis, and hashtags.
- Tag people only from the approved people record.
- Preserve the creator’s edit as feedback; do not automatically generalise from a single correction.
- Require human approval of the media, text, tags, destination, and posting time.

## Inputs ranked by authority

1. Creator’s current notes for the asset
2. Explicit human instructions
3. Approved creator voice examples
4. People and account records
5. Recent published captions
6. Current research findings
7. Embedded image metadata

When inputs conflict, the higher-ranked source wins.
